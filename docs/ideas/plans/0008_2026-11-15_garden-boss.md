# The Garden Heart: Reactions

The second Chapter 3 boss. It is fought in the Garden Heart (`garden_heart`) and introduces the **Reaction** mechanic, which the Fallen King later asks for. Part of [Chapter 3](plan:0006). Before it: [The Court Magician](plan:0007). After it: [The royal quarters boss](plan:0009).

Items here have no complexity badge yet. The implementation plan is `docs/plans/garden-heart-and-reactions.md` (run with `/jp-implement-plan`).

---

## The fight {section}

### Concept

**Status:** design and artbook. The fight is in the Garden Heart (`garden_heart`, the queen's garden `62_47` and the conservatory `63_47`), where today an Alpha Wolf stands on the loop path north of the pond. It is plant-themed and it introduces the **Reaction** mechanic.

The boss is a living plant spread across the garden. Its body is the **Heart**, a node in the ground that is the only part whose damage counts towards the boss's health. Stems grow out of the Heart to other nodes. A node that still has living children is covered by a thick **Shell** and barely takes damage, so the party works from the outside in: kill the tips, and whatever they hung from opens up. When the Heart has lost enough health it closes its Shell, sinks into the ground, and comes up somewhere else with a bigger plant than before.

- **Larger than normal.** The plant reads as a landscape feature, not a unit.
- **Thorns.** The thorn terrain effect with wear stages (`terrainEffectDefs.ts`, `game/terrainEffects/thorns.ts`) is laid by the Creeper.
- **Area control.** Stems block movement and the dead ones become terrain, so the garden's shape changes as the fight goes on.
- **Reactable attacks.** Some of its big, slow attacks are Reactable, so the party can answer them mid-way (see "Where Reactions fit").

### Vocabulary

The names used for the rest of this section and in the art:

- **Heart**: the main body. The only node whose damage reduces the boss's health bar.
- **Knot**: a node that has children (the Heart is the root Knot). Shelled while any child is alive.
- **Bud**: a node with no children, at the end of a Stem. Never shelled. (The brief's "leaf node".)
- **Stem**: the living vine between a node and its parent. Sways, blocks movement, and is dragged along when its Bud moves.
- **Shell**: the thick plating on a Knot. Takes 10 off every hit, down to a floor of 10 (see The Shell).
- **Briar**: a Stem whose Bud has died. Still, and a destructible wall.
- **Tangle**: what a Briar leaves once it is cut down. Very slow ground that stops projectiles and wears down to Grass. (The brief's "thick vines".)

**Decided:** Heart, Knot, Bud, Stem, Shell, Briar, Tangle. **Shell** replaces the proposed Husk, because Husk is already an enemy in the game (`11_enemy_husk`).

### The plant: Heart, Knots, Buds and Stems

- **The tree is data.** Each phase describes a tree: which nodes, what kind each one is, and who is whose parent. The Heart grows the tree when it sprouts.
- **Knots stay put; Buds move.** A Bud wanders on a leash the length of its Stem (about 6 tiles) from its parent, so its Stem is dragged and reshaped as it moves. The Mender Bloom never moves.
- **Stems block movement.** A living Stem is a moving obstacle: units cannot walk through it, so a moving Bud sweeps its Stem across the arena and pushes the party around. Projectiles pass over a living Stem (to be confirmed by play).
- **Stems are not targets** while alive. The way to deal with a Stem is to kill its Bud.
- **Sway.** A living Stem has a slow travelling wave along it (art: `gh-opening`), strongest towards the Bud, so it reads as alive even when the Bud is still.

### The Shell

- **Rule.** A Knot with at least one living child takes 10 less damage from every hit, but a hit never drops below 10 (or below its own damage, if it was already under 10). The Heart is a Knot, so it is shelled whenever anything grows from it.
- **Feedback.** A reduced hit shows its number smaller, grey and drifting slowly, instead of the normal white pop (art: `gh-shell`). The party should learn "this is not working" from the numbers alone.
- **Opening.** When a Knot's last child dies, its Shell opens (the plates split, the core shows) and it takes full damage. It is now a Bud in effect: when it dies, its own Stem becomes Briar and its parent may open in turn.

**Decided:** a shelled hit does at least 10 damage. The Shell takes 10 off a hit, but never takes it below 10, and a hit that was already under 10 lands unchanged. So a 25-damage hit lands as 15, a 14-damage hit as 10, and a 6-damage hit as 6. Builds made of many small hits are not shut out, and the Shell still blunts big hits enough that opening a Knot is worth it. The grey "not working" number shows whenever the Shell took anything off.

### Stems, Briar and Tangle

The life of a Stem after its Bud dies (art: `gh-sever`, `gh-tangle`):

- **Briar.** The Stem stops moving and becomes a wall along the tiles it covered. It blocks movement like the living Stem did. It has health and can be destroyed.
- **Tangle.** A destroyed Briar leaves Tangle on its tiles: very slow ground (much slower than Thick Grass's 0.75) that stops projectiles. Each projectile it stops wears it a little; after 3 it becomes plain Grass.
- **Why this works.** Every Bud the party kills leaves a mark on the arena, so later phases are fought in a garden the party has shaped. They can leave a Briar up as a wall against the Thornspitters, or shoot a Tangle clear to open a lane.

### Phases and the retreat

The Heart's health bar is split into four parts. Each time it drops below a threshold it retreats (art: `gh-retreat-pod`) and resprouts with a bigger plant (art: `gh-phases`).

- **Phase 1 (100% to 75%)**: the Heart and one Bud. A tutorial for the rule: kill the Bud and the Heart opens.
- **Phase 2 (75% to 50%)**: two Buds on the Heart.
- **Phase 3 (50% to 25%)**: two Knots on the Heart, each with one Bud. The first time the party must open a Knot before the Heart.
- **Phase 4 (25% to 0%)**: two Knots with two Buds each, and two more Buds on the Heart.

**The retreat.** The Shell closes, then the Heart shrinks and slides down into the ground, which hides it from the bottom up until it is gone. A burrow trail runs under the ground, and the Heart comes up at a new spot, opens and sprouts the next phase's tree.

- **Untouchable while it retreats.** Damage past the threshold is lost, so a burst cannot skip a phase. The phase system already has an invulnerability window on transitions (`invulnerableSeconds`, `game/units/components/phaseDef.ts`); check that it also clamps the hit that crossed the threshold.
- **What is left behind.** Any living nodes of the old tree die with it, and their Stems become Briar, so the old site stays as terrain.
- **Where it comes up.** The fight spans four map segments, so there is room for each phase to grow on fresh ground. Each segment has authored sprout points, and the Heart comes up at one away from the party and from the old tree's Briar, so a new tree never grows into the last one. The old sites stay as walls and Tangle the party can use or clear.
- **The arena.** `020_garden_heart.ts` stitches two segments today (`62_47_queens_garden`, `63_47_conservatory`). It needs to grow to four; the 2×2 block with `62_48_herb_garden` and `63_48_orchard` is the natural fit.

**Decided:** when the Heart retreats, the old tree dies and its Stems turn to Briar. The four-segment arena gives each phase fresh ground, so the clutter builds up behind the fight rather than on top of it.

### Node kinds

Any node can be any kind, except that the Mender Bloom is never a parent unless a phase says so. The kind is the node's behaviour; being a Knot or a Bud is its place in the tree. A shelled Knot still uses its kind (art: `gh-kit`).

- **Thornspitter (the Shooter).** Fires short bursts of slow thorny projectiles, two quick fans of three, aimed at a player. The answer is to walk between them (art: `gh-shooter`).
- **Pod Mortar (the Bulb Launcher).** Lobs a seed into the ground. It grows for about 5 seconds, shows its blast area in red for the last 1.5 seconds, then bursts (art: `gh-mortar`).
- **Mender Bloom (the Flower).** Rooted, never moves. Heals the other nodes near it, shelled ones and the Heart included. A priority target that is not on the way to the Heart (art: `gh-bloom`).
- **Hive Gourd (the Nest).** Spawns swarmlings and slimes, with a cap on how many it keeps alive (art: `gh-hive`). Reuses the nest spawner (`spawnerCore.ts`, `buildNestSpawnContext`) and the existing `swarmling` and `slime` units.
- **Creeper (the Runner).** Sends out a slow runner vine that lays dark thorny ground as it grows. After 4 to 6 tiles it pulls back; the thorns stay until they wear out (art: `gh-creeper`). The thorns are the existing `dark_thorn` effect (`DARK_THORN_EFFECT_TYPE`).

A first pass at what grows where, so each phase adds one new idea:

- **Phase 1**: one Thornspitter Bud.
- **Phase 2**: a Thornspitter and a Pod Mortar.
- **Phase 3**: a Hive Gourd Knot with a Thornspitter Bud, and a Creeper Knot with a Mender Bloom Bud.
- **Phase 4**: Knots and Buds drawn from all five, with the two extra Heart Buds as Pod Mortars.

**Decided:** the party can destroy a Pod Mortar's seed before it bursts. The seed is a target with fairly high health, 20 base, scaled by the number of players the way most enemy health is, so killing it is a real commitment rather than a free answer. Running and Reacting stay available, and ranged players get a way to answer it from a distance. The seed is a small destructible body, not just a fuse: it needs health, a hit box and a death that cancels the burst (no damage, a short fizzle).

## Reactions in this fight {section}

### Where Reactions fit

This is the fight that teaches Reactions, so some of its attacks are Reactable. Suggested:

- **Pod Mortar burst.** The reaction frame is the start of the 1.5 s red telegraph. A Reaction there is the clearest "answer this now" moment: dash out, raise a shield, pull an ally clear, or burn the seed down (20 base health, scaled by player count) before it bursts.
- **The resprout eruption.** When the Heart comes up it bursts the ground around its sprout point. Reacting there lets a player get clear, or land a free hit before the new tree grows and the Shell closes.
- **Not the Thornspitter.** Its bursts are too frequent; Reacting to them would pause the game all the time.

### The mechanic

A **Reaction** is a short pause the players get in the middle of a boss's attack, in which they can act. It is not the engine's interrupt, which is a crowd-control effect that cancels a unit's cast (`crowdControl/ccInterrupt.ts`, `interruptAllAbilities`, `interruptPolicy` on abilities). A Reaction never cancels the boss's cast by itself.

- **Reflex, the resource.** Each player has a pool of **Reflex**. It is the cap on how often they can React. **Decided:** a player starts each battle with 1 Reflex and recovers 1 per round, up to a cap (start with a cap of 1).
- **Reactable attacks.** Certain boss attacks, such as Crystal Laser, are flagged **Reactable**. A Reactable attack has a **reaction frame**, a moment in its timeline where a Reaction can fire.
- **Knowledge gates it.** A player can only React once they have learned the mechanic, and only while they have at least 1 Reflex. For everyone else a Reactable attack plays out as normal, so the flag can sit on an earlier boss's attack (Crystal Laser) without changing that fight until the party has learned Reactions. **Decided:** the knowledge is granted at the start of the Garden Heart fight. A tutorial pops up the first time Reactions become relevant (the first reaction frame the player qualifies for), even if that is in a later fight.
- **The pause.** When a Reactable attack reaches its reaction frame, the tick becomes a pause plane for every qualifying player **inside the attack's telegraph** (has learned Reactions, has at least 1 Reflex, and is threatened by it). There is no active player at a reaction frame: they are all asked at once, and the plane resolves when all of them have chosen or passed. Reaction frames that fall on the same tick, and any other pause planes on that tick, merge into one plane holding every waiting player.
- **Passing is free.** The player can decline and the attack carries on.
- **Acting costs 1 Reflex.** Choosing an ability in the pause spends 1 Reflex.
- **What acting does.** **Decided:** the chosen ability cancels whatever the player was casting (as `conditionalCancel` does), uses its normal cooldown, and plays out in normal time. The boss's attack keeps running in normal time too.

**Closest thing that exists.** `conditionalCancel` on an ability timing interval (`abilities/abilityTimings.ts`) already pauses mid-cast and offers a choice, but for the player's own cast: pick an eligible ability to cancel it, or wait and it resumes. A Reaction is the same shape (a pause, a pick or a pass) triggered by an enemy's timeline and gated by Reflex. It should reuse that decision flow and its tag filter rather than add a second pause path.

**Where the frame lives.** A reaction frame is a property of an attack's timing, next to `conditionalCancel` on `AbilityTimingInterval`, plus a `Reactable` flag on the ability. Per the engine rule in `minion_battles/AGENTS.md`, build it as a reusable property any boss attack can opt into, not as something the Garden Heart does. The party only needs to be able to see it: the enemy battle timeline (the red danger bars) should mark the frame.

**Boss armour does not get in the way.** Bosses cannot be crowd-control-interrupted while armoured (`canCcInterrupt`), but a Reaction does not need to cancel the boss. A reacting player can still stop an attack by other means (a shield, a dodge, moving out of the telegraph), and cancelling the cast itself stays an optional extension.

### Co-operation: Reacting at another player's pause

Reactions can reward working together. At another player's pause plane (call it Ana's), a different player (Ben) gets a button to say they want to React too. If Ben presses it he gets to choose an ability or pass. Passing is free and acting spends 1 of his own Reflex.

**Decided:** a claim is only allowed while a Reactable attack is in progress (between its start and its hit). A reaction frame already pauses everybody it threatens, so the claim covers the rest: a player outside the telegraph, or a pause plane for someone else's ordinary turn during the attack. The longer-term design is that Reflex buys an extra turn at **any** player's pause plane (see Ideas, Systems). Build the gate as a single rule (`canClaimReaction`) that this version limits to "a Reactable attack is in progress", so widening it later changes the rule and not the claim flow.

This is the dangerous part, because it adds a second player's input to a pause plane that today only waits for its owner. Problems to design out up front:

- **Authority.** The WebRTC mesh is a fast path only. Sync nudges over it carry no order data, and orders come only from the server (see the game-sync-data-flow skill). An "I want to React" message must therefore not change game state by itself. The host validates it (the plane is still open, a Reactable attack is in progress, the claimant has learned Reactions and has at least 1 Reflex) and records it through the claim endpoint, so every client applies it at the same tick.
- **Two claimants at once.** Both claims that reach the host before the plane closes are accepted, and the plane belongs to both. A claim that arrives after the plane has closed is rejected.
- **The plane closing under the request.** Ana passes or acts while Ben's request is in flight. The host handles Ana's order and Ben's claim in one fixed order, so either the claim lands first and the plane stays open for Ben, or the plane closes first and the claim is rejected.
- **Paying only when acting.** Reflex is spent when the ability order is applied, never when the claim is made, so a pass or a timeout costs nothing and a rolled-back order is never half-paid.
- **Pause across reconnects.** A client that reconnects or resyncs mid-plane rebuilds the claim from the engine state, not from the lost WebRTC message. The claim lives in serialized engine state (the pause plane's waiting players).
- **Visibility.** Everyone sees who the plane is waiting for, which claims are pending and which were accepted.

**Decided:** the co-op claim is a **request**, validated by the host.

- **Request.** A player who does not own the plane presses the button. The client broadcasts a Reaction claim request over the WebRTC mesh, to the host and to the other players. The request changes no game state by itself. It carries the plane's id and tick.
- **Others hear it as pending.** The other clients mark the claim as pending and wait for the host's answer. A client that has already played ahead past the plane keeps going, and rewinds to the plane if the claim is accepted.
- **Reply.** The host answers **accepted** or **rejected**, carrying the plane id and tick. It rejects when the plane has already closed, no Reactable attack is in progress, or the claimant has no Reflex or has not learned Reactions.
- **Acceptance is recorded by the host, not submitted as an order.** The host records the claim through a dedicated claim endpoint into the server's game state and the engine state, and only then sends "accepted". The claim is part of state at a known tick and every client applies it identically.
- **One sequence on the host.** The host is the one that closes a plane, so it handles the owner's order and any claims in a single sequence, and it never closes a plane while a claim request for it is still undecided. "Accepted, then the plane resumed anyway" cannot happen.
- **The claimant can stop waiting without a reply.** The claimant abandons its request when it gets a rejection, or when it sees that plane (matched by id) close. A close means the claim lost.
- **Pay only when acting.** Reflex is spent when the ability order is applied, never when the claim is made or accepted. A pass and a rejection cost nothing.

Build order: the single-player Reaction flow first, then this claim flow on top. The pause plane's waiting players are engine state from the start, so the later step is not a rewrite.

### Co-op claim: settled

- **Plane identity.** Every pause plane has an id and a tick. The claim request, the host's reply and the close signal all carry them, and a client only acts on a message whose id matches. Otherwise a close from an earlier pause could make Ben drop a claim that is still live.
- **Timeout.** Not now. A host-driven pause-plane timeout is a system for later (see Ideas, Systems). Until then an accepted claimant who walks away holds the pause like any other waiting player.
- **Dropped messages.** Messages are a shortcut and the truth is state. The host also writes the claim and its outcome to the game state on the server, so every player learns it that way even if a WebRTC message is lost. A lost request is safe because the plane's close ends the claimant's wait.
- **Host's own claim.** The host's claim has no network hop, so it is always in before the plane closes. With several claimants allowed this decides nothing.
- **Mesh link down.** The button is disabled while the link to the host is down. A down link is itself a fault to resolve (see Ideas, Systems).
- **Replays and desync checks.** A rejected claim never touches engine state. An accepted claim is in the recorded stream the engine replays, and the plane's waiting players are in the checkpoint fingerprint, so a desync over a claim is caught.
- **The claim endpoint.** Not `BattleNet.submitOrder`, whose early-return branches (recovering, awaiting ack, client ahead of the host heartbeat) can silently drop an order, and which is built for a player's own orders. A dedicated endpoint, called by the host, writes the claim into the server's game state and the stream the engine replays. Rule: the host never replies accepted until the claim is durably recorded, and a record that cannot be written is a rejection. The exact shape is settled at implementation on architecture grounds (see the jp-plan).

### Settled questions

- **Whose plane is it?** A reaction frame is a plane for every threatened qualifying player, with no active player. A plane can belong to more than one player at once.
- **How much Reflex, and how does it refill?** Start with 1 and recover 1 per round (cap 1 to start). Tune against the Fallen King's Reactable attacks.
- **Which garden attacks are Reactable.** The Pod Mortar burst (frame at the start of the 1.5 s red telegraph) and the resprout eruption.

### Questions still open

- **What a Reaction can use.** Any ability the player has, or only ones tagged for the pause (as `conditionalCancel` filters with `abilityTagFilter`). The jp-plan starts with any ability.

## Engine work {section}

### Engine work this needs

Per "Build the engine, not the feature" (`minion_battles/AGENTS.md`), each piece is a reusable building block:

- **Flat damage reduction.** No per-hit flat reduction exists today (Earth's armour, `abilities/earthCoreArmour.ts`, is a pool that gets used up). Add a generic per-hit reduction a unit can carry, with a condition (here, "while I have living children").
- **Resisted damage numbers.** A variant of the damage number (`damageNumberEffectDef` in `game/effect_defs/textEffects.ts`, which already takes a colour) that is smaller, grey and slower. Shown for any unit whenever a reduction ate most of a hit.
- **Node trees.** A unit component for parent and child links between units, with "shelled while it has children" and "on death, turn my Stem into terrain" as hooks. Tree layouts per phase are data.
- **Tethers (Stems).** A body that links two units: it blocks movement, follows its ends, sways when drawn, and turns into terrain when it dies. Reusable for chains, roots and leashes.
- **Destructible terrain.** Briar needs terrain with health. Tangle needs terrain that wears from projectile hits, the same way thorns count their uses (`THORN_HITS_TAKEN_PARAM`). Tangle could be a variant of Thick Grass (`variantOf`) with its own speed and `blocksProjectiles`.
- **Burrow and resprout.** A phase transition preset: close, sink with the ground hiding it, move to an authored sprout point, rise. Built on the existing phase system (`phaseDef.ts`, `health_below_percent`).
- **Five node kinds.** Each a unit def with its own ability: a burst projectile, a planted seed with a fuse, an area heal, a capped spawner and a runner vine.
- **Destructible seed.** The Pod Mortar's seed is a small unit with 20 base health (scaled by player count like other enemies) that bursts when its fuse ends and is cancelled when it dies. Build it as a generic "fused body" (health, fuse, effect on expiry, effect on death) that other planted things can reuse.
