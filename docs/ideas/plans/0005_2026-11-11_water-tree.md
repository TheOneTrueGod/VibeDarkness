# Water: a new tree, starting with the Blood Mage overlap

Water will be its own tree. For now it is built as an overlap with the Blood Mage: three ice cards that take the place of a Blood Mage card while the caster has Water to pay for them, and fall back to the blood card when they can't. This plan picks the colours, the particle style and the look of the Water bar, shows the icons and effects for the three cards, and lists the engine work.

Each item has a complexity estimate:

- **Low**: a few changes to a few files.
- **Medium**: overhauls a small subsystem.
- **Large**: touches many parts of the code and likely needs a data migration.

**The resource.** Water, max 16. While the caster stands in or next to water and is below max, it draws on up to 4 water tiles in the 3x3 around it: each loses 1/8 of its health per second and the caster gains 0.5 Water per second. Drawing "damages" the water the way hits damage rock; a drained tile drops a level: deep, then water, then shallow, then dirt. Tooltip: **"+0.5/s in or beside water. Drains up to 4 nearby tiles · max 16."**

**The cards.** **Ice Claws** (3 Water, 3 uses) replaces Bloody Claws: the same left, right, X combo, but bigger, reaching further, a little more damage. **Ice Shards** (4 Water) replaces Blood Shard: two throws (the More Rock pattern), each pierces one enemy, slightly more damage, and bleeding enemies still burst blood shards. **Frozen Field** replaces Blood Burst: the classic "high damage, long windup" spell. A 1 s windup, a target point a little way in front of the caster picked 0.2 s before it lands (while ice shards rise there), then a rectangle of ice that deals damage over time and keeps stunning everything inside. A mode toggle switches the card back to Blood Burst.

The implementation plan is `docs/plans/water-tree-blood-mage-overlap.md`.

Baseline: there is no Water theme; the generic theme is `#7dd3fc`. Blue is the most crowded hue in the game: Storm `#3b5bdb` / `#a5f3fc`, Armory `#4f6d8c` / `#e7e5e4`, Training `#7dd3fc`, the Shield resource `#0ea5e9`, Mana `#3b82f6`, Super `#38bdf8`. Darkness owns indigo `#6366f1`, Command owns pine and lime (`#0f766e` / `#d9f99d`), Blood Mage owns red `#f87171`. Water tiles themselves are drawn `#1B4A94` (deep), `#2F7FD6` (water) and `#7DBDF0` (shallow), so the tree has to read against its own terrain. Primary is the **mass** (the core ring, field fills, shard bodies); secondary is the **event** and the resource colour (the bar's surface, claw streaks, frost).

### [x] P0.1 Choose the Water palette

**Complexity:** Low

**Decided:** Tidewater. Primary `#1e3a8a` deep navy, secondary `#5eead4` seafoam.

Once chosen, the pair becomes a new `WATER_THEME` in `themes/abilityThemes.ts` (two-tone from the start, so `svgPalette.test.ts` guards its folder), and the Water resource colour reads the secondary.

### [x] P0.2 Choose the particle style

**Complexity:** Low

**Decided:** the spell circle throws **Ice shards and Droplets** together. Ice Claws' effects and the Ice Shards projectile use the **Frost** trail.

### P0.3 The Water bar

**Complexity:** Medium

A `liquid` bar style on `ResourceDisplay`, beside `pips`, not a Water-only component: the fill's leading edge is a moving surface (two sine layers sliding at different speeds) with a caustic glint and rising bubbles, ticks every 4, and the colours read from the resource. The tooltip gets a generic `tooltipLines` field so the one-line summary above lives on the resource. Shown in the studio's first art piece.

### P0.4 Draw water from terrain

**Complexity:** Medium

Build it as a reusable **terrain siphon**: a resource def names the terrain it draws from (water levels), the radius (the 3x3), the most tiles per second (4), the drain per tile (1/8 health per second) and the gain (0.5/s), and stops at max. Water tiles get a health pool like rock; a drained tile drops one level through a `lowerGround` beside the existing `raiseGround` (`terrain/TerrainType.ts`). Rock or sand resources could reuse it later.

### P0.5 Resource-gated card replacement

**Complexity:** Medium

The swap network (`abilities/abilitySwap.ts`) only activates on a buff today. Add a trigger for "while the caster can pay this card's cost and it has uses left", which swaps the ice card into the blood card's slot, and swaps back the moment either runs out. A caster without the blood card holds the ice card as a normal card (the standalone tree later).

## P1: Good to do

### P1.1 Ice Claws

**Complexity:** Medium

**Decided:** the claw streaks leave the Frost trail. Bloody Claws' combo (left, right, X) with a bigger, further claw built from the same `aimFrameSegmentHitbox`: proposed base (16 forward, 60 side) and tip (92 forward, 26 side) against Bloody Claws' (12, 48) and (62, 18), half-thickness 12 against 8, and 6 damage per claw against 5. 3 Water, 3 uses. Frost chips off each claw, replacing bleed.

### P1.2 Ice Shards

**Complexity:** Medium

**Decided:** the shard flies with the Frost trail. Two throws with their own targets, from the More Rock dual-throw pattern in `card_defs/throwSharedTimings.ts`. Each shard pierces one enemy and stops on the second, 12 damage against Blood Shard's 10. 4 Water. Blood Splinters still fires on release: each bleeding enemy in range bursts a blood shard at the target.

### P1.3 Frozen Field

**Complexity:** Medium

**Decided (2026-11-11):** a high-damage, long-windup spell. A 1 s windup with the Water spell circle at the caster's feet. The target point (a little way in front of the caster) is picked 0.2 s before the first hit, and those 0.2 s play the intro: ice shards rising out of the ground across the field. The field then lands as a rectangle centred on that point, aimed along caster to target, and stays: it deals damage over time and re-stuns every enemy inside on each pulse. A mode strip on the card (`abilityModes.modeAbilityIds`, like Throw Charged Rock) switches the slot to Blood Burst. The studio's Frozen Field piece shows the timing.

### P1.4 Water spell circle and particle def

**Complexity:** Low

Register a `water` spell-circle style that throws ice shards and droplets off its rim, and give the shared particle spec (`game/effects/motes.ts`) the frost, shard and droplet shapes as data, so the circle, the claw streaks and the shard trail all read one def.
