# Chapter 3 bosses and The Fallen King

Chapter 3 has four boss fights, each in a different part of the castle, and each one teaches the party a mechanic they will need for the last. The final fight, in the throne room, is against **The Fallen King**. His abilities are built around three mechanics: super attacks, reactions (stopping an attack midway) and time dilation. The requirement is soft: the party can in principle win without them, but will most likely not have the raw power to. This plan sets the arc, documents the finished Court Magician fight, sketches the garden boss, and holds places for the other two.

Items here have no complexity badge yet, because most of the work is still design. Each boss gets its own section.

---

## Summary {section}

**The arc.** Each boss introduces one mechanic in a fight that is cheap to learn it in, then the Fallen King asks for all three at once.

- **Super attacks**: high burst damage, needed to break through a regenerating shield. Introduced by the Court Magician.
- **Reactions**: a limited resource, Reflex, that lets a player pause a Reactable boss attack at its reaction frame and answer it. Introduced by the garden boss.
- **Time dilation**: not yet fitted in. Introduced by the fourth boss, in the royal quarters.

**The four fights, in the order they are being planned.**

- **The Court Magician**: the wizard's tower (`court_magician_tower`, summoning circle). Built. Introduces super attacks.
- **The garden boss**: the Garden Heart (`garden_heart`, queen's garden and conservatory). A living plant whose Heart is husked while anything grows from it. Introduces Reactions. Designed, with an artbook.
- **The royal quarters boss**: the Royal Bedchamber (`royal_bedchamber`, master bedroom, solar, baths and privy chamber). Introduces time dilation. Not started.
- **The Fallen King**: the throne room (`the_kings_court`, segment `61_44_throne`). The final boss. Needs all three.

**What exists today.** The Garden Heart, Royal Bedchamber and King's Court missions are all placeholders: an Alpha Wolf plus the `castle_boss` escort (`missions/016_the_kings_court.ts`, `020_garden_heart.ts`, `028_royal_bedchamber.ts`). Only the Court Magician has a real setup (`castle/castleBosses.ts`). All four missions are built with `defineCastleMission`, so a new boss is a `boss: { kind: 'custom', ... }` setup, not a new mission class.

**Working rule for The Fallen King.** Each mechanic should make a part of his kit tractable rather than being a hard gate. Brute force stays possible, just very expensive.

## The Court Magician: super attacks {section}

### Overview

**Status:** built (abilities `1401` to `1407` and `1490` in `card_defs/14_enemy_court_magician/`, AI in `game/units/unitAI/courtMagician/`, setup in `castle/castleBosses.ts`).

He keeps his distance and fights through dark crystals that cannot be hurt. They only move or ram when he commands them. He starts with two and conjures one more at each 10% of health lost from 90% down to 50% (five more). Every 10% he also swaps places with the crystal farthest from the party (Mirror Swap), so the party's focus is always pulled away from him. When the party catches up and lands a few hits he blinks across the arena. Below 50% he enrages: denser novas, longer volleys, and the Crystal Laser.

### How the fight works

- **Rearrange** (`1401`): he sweeps a hand and every crystal glides into a new shape: a line pointing at a player, a line across him, a cage round a player, a guard ring round himself, or a wide ring round a player. Each crystal that lands shocks a small area for 3 damage, and the glide previews it.
- **Shard Nova** (`1402`): every crystal flares and bursts a ring of 8 slow shards (12 enraged). Neighbouring crystals are offset by half a step, so the rings interleave into a field to walk through. Where Rearrange just put the crystals decides where the field is thick.
- **Crystal Volley** (`1403`): the crystals nearest a player answer one by one, each doing Crystal Ram (`1404`) at where that player stands when its turn comes. Four crystals, six enraged.
- **Blink** (`1405`) and **Mirror Swap** (`1406`): how he gets away. Swap is cast by the `hpStep` component every 10% lost, and waits while a Crystal Laser is being built, since the laser is made from the crystals.
- **Conjure Crystal** (`1407`): adds a crystal at each of the first five 10% steps.

The loop is that Rearrange decides the shape of the next Shard Nova or Volley, so the party reads the crystals to know what is coming.

### The super: Crystal Laser

This is where the party meets a super attack. Below 50% he casts Crystal Laser (`1490`), and it is shaped differently from everything else he does: it commits time and resources and has a large battlefield effect.

- **Time stops** for 2.2 s while he gathers violet light and every crystal glides into a tight wall in front of him, facing a player. He is exempt from his own Time Stop.
- **Aim**: time resumes with red sight-lines showing the lane, and the party has 1.2 s to react.
- **Fire**: the wall fires one huge laser, a ray from every crystal, for half a round, slowly turning to follow that player. Each ray stops on the first body it meets, burning and pushing it. A raised shield stops it cold and covers everyone behind, so the party wins by taking turns in front with their shields.

> [!FX_PALETTE] Crystal Laser
> primary: #3b0764 Crystal mass
> secondary: #f0abfc Orchid
> art: cm-laser
>
> Aim on the left, fire on the right. The red sight-lines are the telegraph; the shield on the right is the answer.

**What the fight teaches.** The party sees a super from the receiving end: a long, loud commitment with a clear telegraph and a clear answer. It sets up the Fallen King's regenerating shield, where the party has to make the same kind of commitment themselves.

**Open point.** How the party learns to use supers of their own on this boss is not decided here. The fight already tells them what a super looks like, but nothing in it needs one from them.

> [!DECISION] Should the Court Magician require a player super?
> The fight shows a super, but beating him needs none. Options:
> - **Leave it as is.** The party only sees a super. Cheapest, and the Fallen King is the first fight to need one.
> - **Give him a regenerating shield for a short phase.** Breaking it needs burst damage, a small taste of the Fallen King's version. Costs a new shield mechanic that the Fallen King needs anyway.
> - **Make his crystals vulnerable only to burst damage.** Reuses the crystals, but undoes the "cannot be hurt" rule that makes them read as fixtures.
>
> Recommendation: the short shield phase, since it builds the Fallen King's shield early and cheaply.

## The garden boss: Reactions {section}

### Concept

**Status:** design and artbook. The fight is in the Garden Heart (`garden_heart`, the queen's garden `62_47` and the conservatory `63_47`), where today an Alpha Wolf stands on the loop path north of the pond. It is plant-themed and it introduces the **Reaction** mechanic.

The boss is a living plant spread across the garden. Its body is the **Heart**, a node in the ground that is the only part whose damage counts towards the boss's health. Stems grow out of the Heart to other nodes. A node that still has living children is covered by a thick **Husk** and barely takes damage, so the party works from the outside in: kill the tips, and whatever they hung from opens up. When the Heart has lost enough health it closes its Husk, sinks into the ground, and comes up somewhere else with a bigger plant than before.

- **Larger than normal.** The plant reads as a landscape feature, not a unit.
- **Thorns.** The thorn terrain effect with wear stages (`terrainEffectDefs.ts`, `game/terrainEffects/thorns.ts`) is laid by the Creeper.
- **Area control.** Stems block movement and the dead ones become terrain, so the garden's shape changes as the fight goes on.
- **Reactable attacks.** Some of its big, slow attacks are Reactable, so the party can answer them mid-way (see "Where Reactions fit").

### Vocabulary

Proposed names, used for the rest of this section and in the art:

- **Heart**: the main body. The only node whose damage reduces the boss's health bar.
- **Knot**: a node that has children (the Heart is the root Knot). Husked while any child is alive.
- **Bud**: a node with no children, at the end of a Stem. Never husked. (The brief's "leaf node".)
- **Stem**: the living vine between a node and its parent. Sways, blocks movement, and is dragged along when its Bud moves.
- **Husk**: the thick shell on a Knot. Reduces every hit by 10.
- **Briar**: a Stem whose Bud has died. Still, and a destructible wall.
- **Tangle**: what a Briar leaves once it is cut down. Very slow ground that stops projectiles and wears down to Grass. (The brief's "thick vines".)

> [!DECISION] Which names do we use for the plant's parts?
> Options for each term (the first is the suggestion):
> - **Leaf node**: **Bud** (short, says "this one opens"), Bloom (clashes with the healing node), Sprout, Tip.
> - **Branch node**: **Knot** (says "tough, tied"), Burl (more woody, less known), Joint, Node.
> - **Living vine**: **Stem**, Tendril (longer), Vine.
> - **Dead vine wall**: **Briar**, Deadvine, Thornwall.
> - **Dead vine ground**: **Tangle**, Thick vines (plain, but close to Thick Grass), Bramble (taken by the player card Bramble Patch).
> - **Shell**: **Husk**, Shell, Bark.
>
> Recommendation: Heart, Knot, Bud, Stem, Husk, Briar, Tangle. Each is one short word and none clashes with an existing card or terrain name.

### The plant: Heart, Knots, Buds and Stems

- **The tree is data.** Each phase describes a tree: which nodes, what kind each one is, and who is whose parent. The Heart grows the tree when it sprouts.
- **Knots stay put; Buds move.** A Bud wanders on a leash the length of its Stem (about 6 tiles) from its parent, so its Stem is dragged and reshaped as it moves. The Mender Bloom never moves.
- **Stems block movement.** A living Stem is a moving obstacle: units cannot walk through it, so a moving Bud sweeps its Stem across the arena and pushes the party around. Projectiles pass over a living Stem (to be confirmed by play).
- **Stems are not targets** while alive. The way to deal with a Stem is to kill its Bud.
- **Sway.** A living Stem has a slow travelling wave along it (art: `gh-opening`), strongest towards the Bud, so it reads as alive even when the Bud is still.

### The Husk

- **Rule.** A Knot with at least one living child takes 10 less damage from every hit. The Heart is a Knot, so it is husked whenever anything grows from it.
- **Feedback.** A reduced hit shows its number smaller, grey and drifting slowly, instead of the normal white pop (art: `gh-husk`). The party should learn "this is not working" from the numbers alone.
- **Opening.** When a Knot's last child dies, its Husk opens (the plates split, the core shows) and it takes full damage. It is now a Bud in effect: when it dies, its own Stem becomes Briar and its parent may open in turn.

> [!DECISION] Can a husked hit do zero damage?
> A 14-damage hit lands as 4, but a 6-damage hit would land as nothing. Options:
> - **Floor at 0.** Small hits do nothing, which teaches the rule fastest. It hard-counters builds made of many small hits (multi-hit, damage over time).
> - **Floor at 1.** Every hit counts a little, so nothing feels bugged. The grey "1" still says "not working".
> - **A percentage instead of a flat 10.** Fair to every build, but the brief asks for a flat 10.
>
> Recommendation: floor at 1. It keeps the flat 10 and never shows a hit doing nothing.

### Stems, Briar and Tangle

The life of a Stem after its Bud dies (art: `gh-sever`, `gh-tangle`):

- **Briar.** The Stem stops moving and becomes a wall along the tiles it covered. It blocks movement like the living Stem did. It has health and can be destroyed.
- **Tangle.** A destroyed Briar leaves Tangle on its tiles: very slow ground (much slower than Thick Grass's 0.75) that stops projectiles. Each projectile it stops wears it a little; after 3 it becomes plain Grass.
- **Why this works.** Every Bud the party kills leaves a mark on the arena, so later phases are fought in a garden the party has shaped. They can leave a Briar up as a wall against the Thornspitters, or shoot a Tangle clear to open a lane.

### Phases and the retreat

The Heart's health bar is split into four parts. Each time it drops below a threshold it retreats (art: `gh-retreat-pod`) and resprouts with a bigger plant (art: `gh-phases`).

- **Phase 1 (100% to 75%)**: the Heart and one Bud. A tutorial for the rule: kill the Bud and the Heart opens.
- **Phase 2 (75% to 50%)**: two Buds on the Heart.
- **Phase 3 (50% to 25%)**: two Knots on the Heart, each with one Bud. The first time the party must open a Knot before the Heart.
- **Phase 4 (25% to 0%)**: two Knots with two Buds each, and two more Buds on the Heart.

**The retreat.** The Husk closes, then the Heart shrinks and slides down into the ground, which hides it from the bottom up until it is gone. A burrow trail runs under the ground, and the Heart comes up at a new spot, opens and sprouts the next phase's tree.

- **Untouchable while it retreats.** Damage past the threshold is lost, so a burst cannot skip a phase. The phase system already has an invulnerability window on transitions (`invulnerableSeconds`, `game/units/components/phaseDef.ts`); check that it also clamps the hit that crossed the threshold.
- **What is left behind.** Any living nodes of the old tree die with it, and their Stems become Briar, so the old site stays as terrain.
- **Where it comes up.** One of a set of sprout points authored on the garden segments, picked as far as possible from the party.

> [!DECISION] What happens to the old tree when the Heart retreats?
> Options:
> - **It dies and its Stems turn to Briar.** The arena keeps getting more cluttered, which is the point of the terrain mechanic. Phase 4 could get crowded.
> - **It withers away completely.** A clean arena each phase. Loses the "the party shapes the garden" idea.
> - **It stays alive, cut off from the Heart.** The party has to clean up as well as chase. The hardest option, and it blurs which nodes matter.
>
> Recommendation: it dies and becomes Briar, with the sprout points spread out so a new tree never grows into old Briar.

### Node kinds

Any node can be any kind, except that the Mender Bloom is never a parent unless a phase says so. The kind is the node's behaviour; being a Knot or a Bud is its place in the tree. A husked Knot still uses its kind (art: `gh-kit`).

- **Thornspitter (the Shooter).** Fires short bursts of slow thorny projectiles, two quick fans of three, aimed at a player. The answer is to walk between them (art: `gh-shooter`).
- **Pod Mortar (the Bulb Launcher).** Lobs a seed into the ground. It grows for about 5 seconds, shows its blast area in red for the last 1.5 seconds, then bursts (art: `gh-mortar`).
- **Mender Bloom (the Flower).** Rooted, never moves. Heals the other nodes near it, husked ones and the Heart included. A priority target that is not on the way to the Heart (art: `gh-bloom`).
- **Hive Gourd (the Nest).** Spawns swarmlings and slimes, with a cap on how many it keeps alive (art: `gh-hive`). Reuses the nest spawner (`spawnerCore.ts`, `buildNestSpawnContext`) and the existing `swarmling` and `slime` units.
- **Creeper (the Runner).** Sends out a slow runner vine that lays dark thorny ground as it grows. After 4 to 6 tiles it pulls back; the thorns stay until they wear out (art: `gh-creeper`). The thorns are the existing `dark_thorn` effect (`DARK_THORN_EFFECT_TYPE`).

A first pass at what grows where, so each phase adds one new idea:

- **Phase 1**: one Thornspitter Bud.
- **Phase 2**: a Thornspitter and a Pod Mortar.
- **Phase 3**: a Hive Gourd Knot with a Thornspitter Bud, and a Creeper Knot with a Mender Bloom Bud.
- **Phase 4**: Knots and Buds drawn from all five, with the two extra Heart Buds as Pod Mortars.

> [!DECISION] Can the party destroy a Pod Mortar's seed before it bursts?
> Options:
> - **Yes, it has a little health.** A second answer besides running, and it rewards ranged players. More targets on screen.
> - **No, it can only be dodged.** Simpler, and a cleaner fit for a Reaction (see below).
>
> Recommendation: no for the first version. The seed is the natural Reactable attack, and making it destroyable as well gives it two answers before the party has learned the first.

### Where Reactions fit

This is the fight that teaches Reactions, so some of its attacks are Reactable. Suggested:

- **Pod Mortar burst.** The reaction frame is the start of the 1.5 s red telegraph. A Reaction there is the clearest "answer this now" moment: dash out, raise a shield, or pull an ally clear.
- **The resprout eruption.** When the Heart comes up it bursts the ground around its sprout point. Reacting there lets a player get clear, or land a free hit before the new tree grows and the Husk closes.
- **Not the Thornspitter.** Its bursts are too frequent; Reacting to them would pause the game all the time.

### Engine work this needs

Per "Build the engine, not the feature" (`minion_battles/AGENTS.md`), each piece is a reusable building block:

- **Flat damage reduction.** No per-hit flat reduction exists today (Earth's armour, `abilities/earthCoreArmour.ts`, is a pool that gets used up). Add a generic per-hit reduction a unit can carry, with a condition (here, "while I have living children").
- **Resisted damage numbers.** A variant of the damage number (`damageNumberEffectDef` in `game/effect_defs/textEffects.ts`, which already takes a colour) that is smaller, grey and slower. Shown for any unit whenever a reduction ate most of a hit.
- **Node trees.** A unit component for parent and child links between units, with "husked while it has children" and "on death, turn my Stem into terrain" as hooks. Tree layouts per phase are data.
- **Tethers (Stems).** A body that links two units: it blocks movement, follows its ends, sways when drawn, and turns into terrain when it dies. Reusable for chains, roots and leashes.
- **Destructible terrain.** Briar needs terrain with health. Tangle needs terrain that wears from projectile hits, the same way thorns count their uses (`THORN_HITS_TAKEN_PARAM`). Tangle could be a variant of Thick Grass (`variantOf`) with its own speed and `blocksProjectiles`.
- **Burrow and resprout.** A phase transition preset: close, sink with the ground hiding it, move to an authored sprout point, rise. Built on the existing phase system (`phaseDef.ts`, `health_below_percent`).
- **Five node kinds.** Each a unit def with its own ability: a burst projectile, a planted seed with a fuse, an area heal, a capped spawner and a runner vine.

### The mechanic

A **Reaction** is a short pause the players get in the middle of a boss's attack, in which they can act. It is not the engine's interrupt, which is a crowd-control effect that cancels a unit's cast (`crowdControl/ccInterrupt.ts`, `interruptAllAbilities`, `interruptPolicy` on abilities). A Reaction never cancels the boss's cast by itself.

- **Reflex, the resource.** Each player has a pool of **Reflex**. It is the cap on how often they can React.
- **Reactable attacks.** Certain boss attacks, such as Crystal Laser, are flagged **Reactable**. A Reactable attack has a **reaction frame**, a moment in its timeline where a Reaction can fire.
- **Knowledge gates it.** A player can only React once they have learned the mechanic, and only while they have at least 1 Reflex. For everyone else a Reactable attack plays out as normal, so the flag can sit on an earlier boss's attack (Crystal Laser) without changing that fight until the party has learned Reactions. How the knowledge is earned is open: a research node, a trait or a story unlock (see the research-trees skill).
- **The pause.** When a Reactable attack reaches its reaction frame and a player qualifies, the game stops there like any other pause plane (it waits for that player's order, the same way it does for ability selection).
- **Passing is free.** The player can decline and the attack carries on.
- **Acting costs 1 Reflex.** Choosing an ability in the pause spends 1 Reflex.

**Closest thing that exists.** `conditionalCancel` on an ability timing interval (`abilities/abilityTimings.ts`) already pauses mid-cast and offers a choice, but for the player's own cast: pick an eligible ability to cancel it, or wait and it resumes. A Reaction is the same shape (a pause, a pick or a pass) triggered by an enemy's timeline and gated by Reflex. It should reuse that decision flow and its tag filter rather than add a second pause path.

**Where the frame lives.** A reaction frame is a property of an attack's timing, next to `conditionalCancel` on `AbilityTimingInterval`, plus a `Reactable` flag on the ability. Per the engine rule in `minion_battles/AGENTS.md`, build it as a reusable property any boss attack can opt into, not as something the garden boss does. The party only needs to be able to see it: the enemy battle timeline (the red danger bars) should mark the frame.

**Boss armour does not get in the way.** Bosses cannot be crowd-control-interrupted while armoured (`canCcInterrupt`), but a Reaction does not need to cancel the boss. A reacting player can still stop an attack by other means (a shield, a dodge, moving out of the telegraph), and cancelling the cast itself stays an optional extension.

### Co-operation: Reacting at another player's pause

Reactions can reward working together. When another player's pause plane opens at a reaction frame, a **non-active player** gets a button to say they want to React too. If they press it, they get to choose an ability or pass. Passing is free and acting spends 1 of their own Reflex.

This is the dangerous part, because it adds a second player's input to a pause plane that today only waits for the active player. Problems to design out up front:

- **Authority.** The WebRTC mesh is a fast path only. Sync nudges over it carry no order data, and orders come only from the server (see the game-sync-data-flow skill). An "I want to React" message must therefore not change game state by itself. The host has to validate it (the plane is still open, nobody has claimed it, the claimant has learned Reactions and has at least 1 Reflex) and then record the claim through the normal order pipeline, so every client applies it at the same tick.
- **Two claimants at once.** Two players press the button in the same moment. Only one claim can win per pause plane. The rule has to be deterministic (for example, first claim the host accepts, with ties broken by player id), and the loser must see that their press was not taken.
- **The plane closing under the request.** The active player passes or acts while the request is in flight. The host drops a request for a plane that is already resolved, and the claimant sees "too late" rather than a stuck pause.
- **A claim that is never used.** A claimant who walks away would freeze the fight for everyone, as at any pause plane. It needs a timeout that releases the claim, without spending Reflex.
- **Paying only when acting.** Reflex is spent when the ability order is applied, never when the claim is made, so a pass or a timeout costs nothing and a rolled-back order is never half-paid.
- **Pause across reconnects.** A client that reconnects or resyncs mid-plane must rebuild the claim from the engine state, not from the lost WebRTC message. That points to the claim living in serialized engine state (the pause plane's claimant) and not in a client-side flag.
- **Visibility.** The other players need to see whose plane it is and who has claimed it.

> [!DECISION] How is a co-op Reaction claim made safe against races?
> The claim must be one decision every client agrees on. Options:
> - **Host-validated claim.** The non-active player sends a WebRTC message to the host. The host validates it and queues a claim order through the normal order pipeline, and the claim lives in engine state. Fast, and one authority. Needs a timeout and a way to tell the claimant they lost. Recommended.
> - **Claim as an ordinary order.** The button submits a claim order through `BattleNet.submitOrder` like any other order, and the engine decides the winner by tick and player id. No new channel and the most deterministic. Slower, and it inherits the early-return paths in `BattleNet.submitOrder` that can drop an order silently, so the "pressed but nothing happened" case needs handling.
> - **No co-op claim in the first version.** Only the active player Reacts. Safest, and the co-op version follows once the single-player flow is solid.
>
> Recommendation: ship the single-player Reaction flow first, then add the host-validated claim. Either way, build the pause plane's claimant as engine state from the start, so the later step is not a rewrite.

### Questions still open

- **Whose plane is it?** At a reaction frame, does every qualifying player get a pause in turn, or does the plane belong to one player (the boss's target)? The co-op button above assumes a plane belongs to one player.
- **How much Reflex, and how does it refill?** A fixed pool per battle, per round or on a timer, and whether it can be gained from good play. This sets how often the mechanic can be used and should be tuned against the Fallen King's Reactable attacks.
- **What a Reaction can use.** Any ability the player has, or only ones tagged for the pause (as `conditionalCancel` filters with `abilityTagFilter`).
- **Which garden attacks are Reactable**, and where in each one the frame sits. Suggested above: the Pod Mortar burst and the resprout eruption.

## Suggested order {section}

- **First**: finish the Court Magician section by answering whether it needs a player super.
- **Then**: the garden boss. Settle the co-op Reaction claim rule first, because every other garden design choice depends on them.
- **Then**: the royal quarters boss, which settles what time dilation does.
- **Last**: The Fallen King, once all three mechanics have a working example.
